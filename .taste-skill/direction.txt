This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
The name is the headline. Set it so large that it becomes architecture: as wide as the viewport, cropped by an edge, or stacked to fill the screen. Nothing else on the first screen competes; the description of what this is drops to a single small sentence. The idea comes from treating the name's letters as material. Ask what the subject can do to its own name: a photograph of the work sits inside a counter or replaces a letter, the name repeats down the page like a reel, the letters are split to opposite corners and the page happens between them. In the study this was the single most common device among sites scoring 7.5 or more.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: the wordmark spans 88–100% of the viewport width (font-size 16–24vw, set with a clamp so it never wraps), anchored either to the bottom edge with its descenders nearly touching it, or dead centre with everything else pushed to the corners. One line of supporting text at 16–20px sits in a corner on a shared margin; navigation is three or four words in the opposite corner. After the hero the page is deliberately small and calm: a narrow text column or a simple two-column grid, so the contrast with the monument holds. The name returns once more at full width as the footer, in the inverse colours. Margins are tight (20–32px) so the type reaches the edges. Two type sizes do nearly all the work: the monument and a small text size, with one mid size for section headings.

## 3. Type — Panchang / Switzer
A flat-sided extended grotesk in heavy capitals, wide enough that two words fill the screen, with a neutral sans kept small. Suits portfolios, real estate, travel and brand sites that want a broad, confident wordmark.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://api.fontshare.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://cdn.fontshare.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=panchang@700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=switzer@400,500&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Panchang** — weight 700 · tracking -0.02em · leading 1.0 · uppercase; four words or fewer per line because the face is very wide
- Text: **Switzer** — 400 for body, 500 for emphasis · leading 1.5 · tracking 0
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
One family. The display cut must survive enormous sizes: tight tracking (-0.04 to -0.07em), leading 0.8–0.9, optical kerning on. Section headings are modest (28–40px) on purpose.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

## 4. Colour — Night and cornflower
```css
:root {
  --bg: #0a0d14;
  --surface: #131824;
  --ink: #e9edf3;
  --muted: #8891a1;
  --accent: #5a9bfc;
  --accent-ink: #0a0d14;
}
```
Blue-black ground and slate panels with silver-white text fill the page; no section changes colour. Cornflower blue marks the buttons and at most one word in a heading.
Two values carry the page (ground and ink). The accent, if used at all, appears on one thing only: the single action or the inset image frame. The footer inverts ground and ink.

## 5. Imagery
**The project has no photographs of its own yet.** First choice: **real pictures of the subject's world from the open collections**: its city and street in old postcards and photographs, an old map of the neighbourhood, its materials up close, its trade in engravings, museum objects of its kind (references/imagery.md §3). Start with `node /workspace/.cursor/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "studio portfolio"`, then queries from your inventory (its street, its materials, its trade's tools and history). Download what you use into the project with `--get` and credit every one on the page (`--credits` prints the footer line). They show the world around the client, never the client's own room, dish, product, person or team. Second, for what no honest picture exists of: draw the subject's own things in code, large, in one flat graphic language and the palette's real colours (its tiles, its windows, its objects; references/imagery.md §8). Third: the dealt photographs, only where an honestly generic picture fits (the sea, the weather, a material), never one a visitor could take for the thing itself and never an unrelated scene. One treatment for all of them. If this direction is built on photographs, plan the set before building (four to six pictures of one family) and reuse them at different crops and scales instead of finding or drawing a new picture for every slot; keep drawings as `<symbol>` + `<use>` or generate them with a small script so the page stays light. List every found picture (with its source and licence), every dealt photograph and every drawing that stands in for photography in your report.

Few images, used as inserts in the typography rather than as illustrations beside it: one photograph inside or behind the name (a 4:5 or 1:1 frame replacing a letter, or tucked between two words), then a short run of large full-width or half-width photographs further down. Square corners. No collage of small thumbnails.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

## 6. Hero — Title from the top edge to the bottom edge
One full-bleed photograph or film at 100vw by 100svh, with a horizon near half height and empty sky or darkness above. The headline is two lines in a heavy face at clamp(140px, 13vw, 200px), leading 0.85, pulled as far apart as the screen allows: line one flush to the 32px left margin with its cap line 72px from the top; line two indented 28vw (403px) and anchored to the bottom, its baseline 24px above the lower edge, running towards the right margin. Lay the hero out as a grid of three rows (`grid-template-rows: auto 1fr auto`): line one in the first row, line two in the third, both spans of one `h1` with `display: contents`. The middle row leaves the picture clear apart from one supporting sentence at 18px, max 44ch, crossing the horizon at 48% of the height from 30vw, and the single action directly beneath it as a solid rectangular button with its label at 18px. The wordmark and two text links sit top-right on the 32px margin at 15px; the top-left belongs to line one. Choose a picture whose upper-left and lower-right are calm enough for the type, or lay a neutral scrim under those two corners only.

On a phone: Keep the two lines at 19vw: line one flush left on a 16px margin 88px down, line two right-aligned to the 16px margin with its baseline 20px above the bottom. The sentence at 17px and the action button sit between them on the left margin, at 46% of the height. The picture is re-cropped to portrait around the horizon.
Headline: Two words or short phrases, one per line, at most about 8 characters each.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. work — Names as one paragraph (statement)
All project or client names run together as one paragraph at poster scale: 10–18 names at clamp(40px, 5.2vw, 78px), leading 1.1, flowing inline across the full width between 24px margins and wrapping where they fall, five to seven lines in all. Names are separated by a comma, or by a small inline frame: an `inline-block` 0.8em tall and 1.2em wide, aligned to the cap height, holding that project's photograph and placed directly after its name. Every name is a link; all names rest in the soft ink, and the hovered one takes full ink while its inline frame scales to 1.6 times from its left edge. A heading of two words opens the paragraph as its first words in full ink, on the same line and at the same size, followed by a colon. Below the paragraph at the left margin, one line at 15px gives the count and the years covered.
On a phone: The paragraph is set at 9vw, left-aligned, about twelve lines, with names separated by commas. Inline frames are removed except after the first three names, where they stay at cap height. All names are full ink; each remains a link with 6px of extra line spacing for touch.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

### 2. pricing — Plan names, one live figure (index)
Plans are a list of names; the price shown belongs to whichever name is selected. Columns 1–6 hold the plan names stacked at about 5vw, leading 1.1, each a `<button>`; the selected one is in the full ink, the others in the softer ink, and the selected name carries a solid dot 0.3em across at its left. Columns 8–12 hold one stage that stays in place while its contents change: the selected plan's price at about 9vw at the top, the period beside it at 18px, a paragraph of three or four lines at 18px saying what that plan includes, and one rectangular button whose label names the plan. Changing plan swaps the stage contents with a 200ms crossfade; the figure's position never moves. Selection follows click and keyboard focus. The middle plan is selected on load. There is no heading: the names are the heading.
On a phone: Plan names stack at 10vw, each on its own line. Tapping one opens its stage directly beneath that name (price at 20vw, paragraph at 17px, full-width button) and closes the others, like a large accordion without lines. The middle plan starts open.

### 3. about — Pinned title, portraits pass (pinned)
A wrapper tall enough for its portraits: about 90svh per person, up to six people. A `position: sticky; top: 0` layer of 100svh holds the heading, two words at clamp(88px, 12vw, 180px), leading 0.85, centred at mid-height, in white with `mix-blend-mode: difference` so it reads over ground and photographs alike. Beneath it in the stacking order, portraits scroll past in normal flow: each a 4:5 photograph 26–30vw wide, alternately starting at column 2 and ending at column 11, 60svh apart, so each one passes behind a different half of the heading. Under each portrait's left corner: the name at 18px, the role at 14px in the soft ink, and one sentence of up to 30 words in a 280px measure. The last portrait is followed by 40svh of empty ground before the heading releases. Square corners. Nothing else is on screen.
On a phone: The heading shrinks to 17vw and pins 16px from the top instead of at mid-height. Portraits become 72% wide, alternating between the left and right margins, 48px apart, each with name, role and sentence beneath at the portrait's width.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names colours (white): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).
- It blends text with `difference`: over anything but black, white or grey that gives random hues. Use a solid ink chosen for each ground instead.

### 4. how-it-works — Run-in column (list)
The quietest sequence: one reading column and good typography. A single column 520–600px wide starts at about 30% of the viewport width (or sits on the page's text axis). The heading is a two-to-five-word question or statement at 28–32px, 48px above the first paragraph. Three to five paragraphs follow at 17–18px with line-height 1.6. Each opens with a bold run-in phrase in full ink that names its moment ('To begin,', 'Within a day,', 'After that,', 'From then on,') while the rest of the paragraph runs in the soft ink; there are no subheads, bullets or markers. After each paragraph, 20px lower, comes one small piece of evidence exactly as wide as the column: a cropped interface fragment, a photograph at 16:9, or a row of three small frames 96px tall. 72px separates stages. Nothing in the section is wider than the column, and the space to its left stays empty.
On a phone: The column fills the width between 20px margins. Type stays at 17px. Evidence pieces keep the column width; the row of three frames becomes a single sideways-scrolling row 88px tall. 56px between stages. The heading drops to 24px.

### 5. faq — Large questions, answers beside (table)
Every answer is open; nothing collapses. Each question-and-answer pair is a row on the 12-column grid: the question in columns 1–6 at clamp(26px, 2.6vw, 40px), medium weight, leading 1.1, no more than two lines; its answer in columns 8–12 at 17–18px with leading 1.55 and a measure near 40ch, its first baseline level with the question's first baseline. Rows are separated by 72–88px of empty space and nothing else. Five to seven rows. The section heading, two or three words, is set at the same size as the questions but in the softer ink and sits as the first row's left cell with an empty right cell, so it reads as the top of the same table rather than a title above it. Because questions are large and answers small, the left column works as a scannable index.
On a phone: Each pair stacks: question at 24px, answer 12px beneath at 17px, then 56px of space before the next. The heading leads at the same 24px in the softer ink. Answers stay open; there is no accordion on the phone either.

### 6. contact — Address in display rows (list)
The contact block is a stack of five full-width rows, every one set at the same size, clamp(44px, 6vw, 88px), regular weight, left-aligned with a 20px inset and leading 1.0: the building or venue name, the street, the city with postcode, the opening hours as one compact line, the phone number. Each row is about 150px tall with the text sitting in its lower third; rows are told apart by alternating two flat tones of the section colour as full-width bands, or by 28px of space alone. A sixth row holds a one-word greeting at the same size followed, 24px to its right and centred on the x-height, by a solid round button 0.9em across with an arrow, which opens the email or booking link. Street and phone rows are links (map, call). No heading and no form. Optionally, one small round photograph sits at the right end of the first row.
On a phone: Rows at 9.6vw with 16px insets, about 84px tall; the hours break onto two rows (days, then times). The greeting row becomes a full-width band in the accent colour with the round arrow button at its right end, 56px across.

## 8. Motion — Masked headline
On load, each line of the headline rises from behind its own baseline, about 900ms with a strong ease-out, the second line 110ms after the first; the supporting sentence and the action then fade up together. Nothing else on the page performs. Because headlines are two lines at most, you author the line breaks and wrap each line yourself. Recipe: 'Masked line reveal' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Scale. The size of the name against the smallness of everything else is the entire visual event, so protect both ends: do not shrink the name to make room, and do not let body text creep above 20px.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
A long name set small is just a logo. If the name has more than about 12 characters, stack it on two lines or crop it with the viewport edge; never drop below 12vw. Do not add a second big headline under the name. Do not decorate the letters.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
